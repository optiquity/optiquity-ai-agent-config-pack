  **Provenance.** BD-288 shipped four waves and resolved on 2026-08-24. This entry re-opens them against v11.1 with their design intact.
  **W3 — the bareness coverage gap (new Check 95).** Two checks gate cross-references for *bareness* (a filename reference carrying no directory qualifier): Check 40 walks a non-recursive `pack-ops/*.md` glob, and Check 43 walks the client-installed surface. Between them they leave a hole. Measured: the live uncovered surface is **35 files** — the 8 non-client-installed `supporting-docs/` files, the repo-root `README.md`, the pack-root trinity, and the per-CLI agent and skill definitions. Check 95 covers it by walk-subtraction (Check 40's walk and Check 43's walk are both subtracted out, so the exemption sets stay disjoint by construction and no `_CHECK_95_ALLOWLIST` entry can ever exempt anything under `pack-ops/`). The census is complete: **122 occurrences triaged individually, 77 KEEP over 26 basenames, 45 STRIP over 11**, every verdict recorded in `maintenance-docs/v11-implementation/BD-288-c95-triage.json`. The allowlist is **34 entries** — the 26 measured KEEP basenames plus the 8 that Check 40's tier-1 allowlist clears and Check 95's does not — sized by leave-one-out to zero redundancy, necessity sum 189 closing exactly against the empty-allowlist probe.
  **The one trap this wave must not repeat.** BD-288's adversarial planner found, by building W3 in a scratch tree and running the battery, that the guard and its 45-occurrence fix-set are **one atomic change**. Committing Check 95 before the qualifications land yields `rc=1` with 45 failures, because the STRIP set is by construction exactly what the allowlist does not clear. The plan merges them into a single commit for that reason.
  **W5 — operating-doc truthfulness repair.** Three sites in pack operating docs state something untrue about how RO pack agents are invoked and about what the Codex `workspace-write` sandbox grant does. A reader would act on them and get an error. This is the residue of the v11.0 investigation into whether the Codex sandbox could be mechanically narrowed: it cannot (`writable_roots` cannot narrow to a deliverable path that lies outside the workspace, and no pack-side `.codex/config.toml` exists to carry it), and the grant is in any case inert on this repo because nothing registers those agent files with Codex. The narrowing was measured and rejected on evidence; what survives is the falsehood, which is itself an instance of this entry's defect class.
  **W6 — the non-discriminating test-assertion guard (new Check 96).** A per-check test can assert on a substring that appears in BOTH its check's FAIL output and its WARN output, so the assertion cannot discriminate and contributes nothing while a neighbouring clause carries the load. One live instance remains. The measured population is **2**, not the 51 an early estimate suggested — 51 counts files using the assertion *shape*, and the defect only bites when the chosen substring is non-discriminating. Check 96 detects the class and **ships with no allowlist constant at all**, because the measured legitimate KEEP set is empty. Two guard designs were measured and rejected first: the naive form has a 50% miss rate, and a substring-must-exist form has roughly 70% false positives.
  **Why this is one entry and not three.** All three are the same defect class, all three were designed together, and W6's encoding surfaces overlap W3's (registry, ledger, count claims) so they must move in lock-step. Splitting them would re-open the sequencing question the plan already answered.
